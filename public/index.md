## Which parts of Farnborough are covered?

Farnborough grew from a small village into a railway and military town in the 19th century, then expanded again in the 1960s and 1970s. That history leaves a wide spread of roofs: Victorian and Edwardian villas near the station, large post-war estates, and newer infill. Each needs a slightly different approach to gutter clearing.

**South Farnborough**  
Conservation area (designated 1987) between Farnborough Road, Alexandra Road, Lynchford Road and Reading Road. Large period houses, many with mature protected trees in the gardens.

**Farnborough Street and Farnborough Hill**  
Two conservation areas designated in 2025. Older buildings with steeper roofs, and slate or clay tiles that shed moss and grit into the gutter.

**North Camp**  
Streets close to the garrison boundary with a mix of terraces and semi-detached houses on tight plots. Access to the back of the house is often through a narrow side passage.

**Cherrywood, West Heath and Fernhill**  
Mainly post-war and later estate housing. These are usually two-storey uPVC systems with a standard run length.

**Town centre flats and maisonettes**  
Low-rise blocks where the gutter may be managed by a landlord or managing agent rather than the occupier.

## What do Farnborough homes mean for their gutters?

### Victorian and Edwardian

The town's focus moved to the railway station area in the mid-19th century, and South Farnborough still keeps many large villas from that era. Expect deep eaves, original cast iron on some houses, and long gutter runs.

### 1960s and 1970s

Farnborough and Aldershot were both expanded and redeveloped in the 1960s and 1970s, including a Greater London Council overspill estate. Most of these homes now have replacement uPVC half-round or square gutters.

### Recent infill

Newer flats and town houses can be three storeys. Height and access drive the price more than gutter length.

### Trees and water

Tree-work applications in South Farnborough regularly involve protected pines and oaks in garden groups, for example on Albert Road. Pine needles knit into a dense mat that holds water and keeps holding it, while oak leaves arrive in one heavy autumn drop.

Cove Brook and the Basingstoke Canal run through the west and south of the town as green corridors.

### Problems we see most in Farnborough

- Pine needle mats on period houses near protected garden trees, which often overflow at the corners first
- Moss and grit washing off older concrete and clay tile roofs on the 1960s estates
- Downpipes on terraces that discharge into shared gullies and block at the shoe
- Leaking joints on uPVC systems that are now 30 years old or more

## Common questions

### How much does gutter cleaning cost?

Typical local prices run from about £65 to £100 for a terraced house, £80 to £125 for a semi and £110 to £190 for a detached house, depending on access and build-up. The price estimator gives a closer range for your home.

### How often should gutters be cleaned?

Once a year for most homes, ideally after leaf fall. Homes under oak, horse chestnut or pine usually need two visits a year.

### Who does the work?

Your enquiry goes to one vetted gutter specialist covering your area, who replies with a price and carries out the work. Your details aren't shared with anyone else.

### Do I need to be home?

Usually not. If side gates can be left unlocked, the gutters can be cleared and the photos sent afterwards.

### How is the price confirmed?

You describe your home in the form and get a confirmed price by email before anything is booked. If a repair is found during the visit, it is photographed and priced separately, never added without your agreement.

### Do I need permission for gutter work in South Farnborough conservation area?

Cleaning and like-for-like repair don't change how a house looks, so they don't need consent. Replacing gutters with a different material or profile on a house in a conservation area is a different matter, and on a listed building it can need consent. Check with Rushmoor Borough Council before changing anything visible.

### My neighbour's tree overhangs my gutter. What can I do?

Clearing your own gutters is always fine. If the tree is protected by a Tree Preservation Order or stands in a conservation area, any cutting back needs notice to Rushmoor, and the six-week notice rule applies in conservation areas.
