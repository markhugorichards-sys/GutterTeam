## Which parts of Frimley are covered?

Frimley combines a historic village core with Edwardian streets and large 1950s to 1970s estates that grew together with Frimley Green and Mytchett along the Blackwater Valley. Most homes are two-storey detached or link-detached houses, and many back gardens run down towards the wooded valley edge.

**Frimley**  
Historic core around the High Street, Edwardian terraces and semis, and large post-war estates of detached and link-detached homes.

**Frimley Green**  
Edwardian plots along the Blackwater Valley and post-war estates around the green.

**Mytchett**  
Post-war estates on former farmland. Back gardens along the Blackwater Valley create a wooded backdrop, and the Basingstoke Canal runs through.

**Deepcut edge**  
Homes on the edge of the sandy woodland around Deepcut.

## What are Frimley homes and gutters like?

### Edwardian

Smaller plots, often terraced or semi-detached. Rear access can be tight.

### 1950s to 1970s

The main estates: detached and link-detached houses where the link roof often has its own short gutter.

## How do Frimley’s trees affect your gutters?

The valley-side gardens and sandy woodland mean a mix of broadleaf leaf fall and pine needles, depending on which side of Frimley you're on.

The River Blackwater runs along the western side. The Basingstoke Canal passes through Mytchett.

### What problems are most common in Frimley?

- Short link-roof gutters between link-detached houses, which block quickly
- Leaf and needle build-up on homes backing onto the valley woodland
- Ageing uPVC joints on 1960s and 1970s systems

**Best time to book in Frimley:** Clean once a year after autumn leaf fall. Add a spring visit if you back onto the valley woodland.
