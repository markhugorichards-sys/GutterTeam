## Which parts of Fleet are covered?

Fleet was mostly heathland until the railway arrived in 1847. The town grew after the 1880s, when the land around Fleet Road was mapped out into building plots. That gives Fleet an unusually large Edwardian core, ringed by mid and late 20th-century estates and the 2000s development at Elvetham Heath. Mature trees line much of the town.

**North Fleet**  
Conservation area since 1994, with an Article 4 direction since 1998. Large Edwardian houses in well-treed grounds.

**Pondtail**  
Mostly detached homes, with numerous mature trees on Dunmow Hill, Chestnut Grove, Wellington Avenue and Wood Lane, and woodland on the edge of Fleet Pond.

**Elvetham Heath**  
About 1,850 homes built roughly 2000 to 2008, including two- and three-storey apartment blocks, next to a 20 ha nature reserve.

**Ancells Farm**  
Mainly detached two-storey brick houses from the 1970s onwards on curving cul-de-sacs, with mature trees in front gardens.

**West Fleet**  
Mostly 1950s and later housing. Tavistock Road has many mature trees and wide verges.

**South Fleet**  
Mature trees along Albany Road and Aldershot Road, semi-natural woodland at Oakley Park, and the canal to the south.

**Town centre streets**  
Edwardian terraces and semis on Albert, Clarence and Connaught Roads, in brown brick with slate roofs.

## What are Fleet homes and gutters like?

### Edwardian

Fleet's core was developed largely in the early 20th century. Slate roofs, steep pitches and decorative gable fascias are common, and some houses may still have original cast iron.

### Mid to late 20th century

Cul-de-sac estates across Pondtail, Ancells, West Fleet and South Fleet, mostly two-storey detached homes with concrete tile roofs.

### 2000s

Elvetham Heath's homes include three-storey blocks where reach equipment matters.

## How do Fleet’s trees affect your gutters?

Fleet's housing studies keep coming back to mature trees: rear-garden tree lines forming green corridors in Pondtail, woodland around Fleet Pond and the North Hants golf course, and street trees across North Fleet. Many Fleet roofs sit under a canopy.

Fleet Pond, the largest freshwater lake in Hampshire, sits on the north side of the town. The Basingstoke Canal runs along the southern edge and separates Fleet from Church Crookham.

### What problems are most common in Fleet?

- Heavy autumn leaf loads in North Fleet and Pondtail, often needing two visits a year
- Slate debris and moss from older Edwardian roofs
- Three-storey access at Elvetham Heath
- Gutter guards clogging under pine needles. Guards cut leaf build-up but still need checking

**Best time to book in Fleet:** Many Fleet homes are under trees, so plan on two cleans a year: one after leaf fall in December and one in spring. Homes on open estates can usually manage with one.

## Fleet questions

### I live in North Fleet. Does the Article 4 direction affect gutter work?

Cleaning and like-for-like repairs don't change the look of your house. An Article 4 direction removes some permitted development rights, so check with Hart District Council before changing gutter material, colour or profile on a front elevation.

### Is Elvetham Heath covered?

Yes. Elvetham Heath, Ancells Farm and every part of GU51 and GU52 are inside the coverage area.

### Do I need to be home?

Usually not. If side gates can be left unlocked, the gutters can be cleared and the photos sent afterwards.
