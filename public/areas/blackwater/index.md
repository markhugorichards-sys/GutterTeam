## Which parts of Blackwater and Hawley are covered?

Blackwater, which takes in the historic hamlets of Frogmore, Darby Green and Hawley, sits where the old A30 crosses the River Blackwater. The river runs the full length of the town. Most of its historic buildings date from about 1890 to 1910, but 1960s to 1980s estates now make up most of the housing.

**Blackwater North and Frogmore**  
Late 19th and early 20th-century cottages, 1960s terraces, detached homes, bungalows and chalet bungalows. Notable tree groups along Frogmore Road and around The Green.

**Darby Green**  
A conservation area with woodland and wooded surroundings.

**Hawley**  
Historic farmsteads and cottages, 1950s and 1960s estates, and thick woodland around Waldorf Heights, with views over Hawley Common.

## What are Blackwater and Hawley homes and gutters like?

### 1890 to 1910

Cottages and some larger houses, often with slate and older gutter profiles.

### 1960s to 1980s

About 80% of Blackwater North and 70% of Hawley. These are standard uPVC systems with ageing seals.

## How do Blackwater and Hawley’s trees affect your gutters?

Mature trees and hedging line Hawley Road, Vicarage Road and Green Lane, and roads such as Fir Drive and Birch Drive are named for what grows over them. Expect a mix of pine needles and birch seed.

The River Blackwater forms the north-eastern boundary, and some homes back onto its valley.

### What problems are most common in Blackwater and Hawley?

- Pine and birch debris around Hawley and Waldorf Heights
- Chalet bungalow dormer gutters in Blackwater North
- Ageing uPVC joints on 1960s systems

**Best time to book in Blackwater and Hawley:** Clean once each winter. Homes under the pines around Hawley usually need a spring check too.
