## Which parts of Hook are covered?

Hook is one of the newer urban areas in Hart District. It grew significantly from a historic village core on London Road and Station Road, with large estates added to the north, east and west from the 1980s onwards. The River Whitewater runs through East Hook, and Hook Common lies to the south of the old centre.

**Historic core**  
Cottages, timber-framed farmhouses and Edwardian terraces on Newnham Road, with Welsh slate and even thatch.

**North Hook**  
Large detached houses on Elms Road under tall mature trees, inter-war bungalows and a 1980s estate.

**East Hook**  
Holt Lane's older buildings, 1960s and 1990s estates, Felders Mede's three-storey homes, and the River Whitewater meadows.

**West Hook**  
Late 1970s to 2000s houses near Great Sheldon Coppice.

**South Hook**  
Post-war bungalows and semis around Rectory Road, plus 1990s detached homes.

## What are Hook homes and gutters like?

### Historic

Slate, plain tile and thatch in the old core. Gutter details vary from building to building.

### 1940s to 1970s

Bungalows with hipped roofs and modest estate houses.

### 1980s onwards

Most of Hook's homes, mainly two-storey with concrete tile roofs, and some three-storey buildings at Felders Mede.

## How do Hook’s trees affect your gutters?

Hook has a horse chestnut avenue on Dorchester Road, tall mature trees on Elms Road, an oak copse at Birch Grove and Great Sheldon Coppice to the north-west. Horse chestnut leaves are large and wet and pack down hard in a gutter.

The River Whitewater flows through East Hook, past the water meadows at Austen's Meadow.

### What problems are most common in Hook?

- Large, wet horse chestnut and oak leaves packing gutters in late autumn
- Hipped bungalow roofs with gutters on all four sides
- Three-storey access at Felders Mede

**Best time to book in Hook:** Clean once after leaf fall. Homes on Elms Road and near the coppices benefit from a second visit in spring.
