## Which parts of Church Crookham are covered?

Church Crookham lies south of Fleet, with the Basingstoke Canal as the traditional dividing line. A few late Victorian and Edwardian houses survive, but most buildings date from the 1960s or later. They include the large estate built on the former Queen Elizabeth Barracks after the Gurkhas left in 2000.

**Zebon Copse estate**  
Built around 20 acres of broadleaved woodland next to the canal. Homes backing onto the copse get a heavy autumn leaf load.

**Former Queen Elizabeth Barracks site**  
Modern housing on the old barracks land. These are newer uPVC systems, often two and three storeys.

**Basingbourne and Gally Hill**  
Older roads with some of the area's surviving period houses and mature trees.

**Crookham Village edge**  
Lower-density homes towards the older village to the west.

Late 19th and early 20th-century houses survive among the later estates.

### 2000s and later

The barracks redevelopment added modern homes, including three-storey town houses.

## How do Church Crookham’s trees affect your gutters?

Zebon Copse is mixed deciduous woodland, with plants that indicate woodland here since at least the 16th century. The wider area has a history of copse enclosures and woodland. Expect oak and other broadleaf leaf fall in late autumn.

The Basingstoke Canal and its conservation area run along the northern edge.

### What problems are most common in Church Crookham?

- Autumn leaf loads on homes next to Zebon Copse and the canal tree lines
- Three-storey access on the newer estates
- Ageing seals on 1960s and 1970s uPVC systems

**Best time to book in Church Crookham:** Book after leaf fall in late November or December. Homes next to the copse usually need a second visit in spring.
