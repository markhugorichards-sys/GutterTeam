## Which parts of Hartley Wintney are covered?

Hartley Wintney combines an 18th-century coaching town on the A30 with a village set around wooded and open greens. Its best-known feature is the regimented lines of oak trees on the Common. Most of the historic core is in a conservation area, with 20th-century neighbourhoods to the north and south.

**Village centre and the Common**  
The conservation area core around the High Street and the oak-lined Common.

**Hartley Wintney North**  
A Victorian terrace on Chapel Row, inter-war houses, 1950s bungalows and 1960s to 1970s estates. Lime trees line Brackley Avenue.

**Hartley Wintney South and Phoenix Green**  
Victorian and Edwardian houses at Phoenix Green, inter-war and early post-war housing, and later cul-de-sacs.

**West Green**  
Mixed woodland and small fields to the west.

## How do Hartley Wintney’s trees affect your gutters?

Oak defines Hartley Wintney. The Common's oaks drop leaves and acorns through October and November, and the lime trees on Brackley Avenue add seed in summer. Woodland at Hazeley Heath lies to the north.

The village sits on a ridge between the valleys of the River Hart and the River Whitewater.

### What problems are most common in Hartley Wintney?

- Heavy oak leaf and acorn loads in late autumn near the Common
- Lime seed and sticky deposits along tree-lined avenues
- Mixed and older gutter profiles in the conservation area

**Best time to book in Hartley Wintney:** Wait until the oaks have finished dropping, usually early December, then clean. Clearing too early often means a second blockage before Christmas.
