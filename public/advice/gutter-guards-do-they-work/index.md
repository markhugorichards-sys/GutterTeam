# Do gutter guards work?

Some do, under the right trees. None stop cleaning altogether. The type of debris over your roof decides which guard helps and which one makes blockages harder to clear.

Updated 12 November 2026

## The short answer

| Guard type | Good against | Struggles with | Still needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Micro-mesh (fine stainless mesh on a frame) | Leaves, pine needles, grit | Matted debris sitting on top during leaf fall | Brushing off the top once or twice a year |
| Standard plastic or metal mesh | Large leaves such as oak and horse chestnut | Pine needles and seeds, which thread through; cheap mesh sags | A clean each winter, often lifting the mesh |
| Brush (bottle-brush in the gutter) | Large leaves on open estates | Needles, catkins and moss, which lodge in the bristles | Lifting out and shaking the brushes, at least yearly |
| Foam inserts | Large leaves, short term | Silt, seeds and moss grow into the foam | Replacement every few years |
| Solid or reverse-curve covers | Leaves on shallow roofs | Heavy rain overshooting, and needles slipping into the slot | Annual check |

## Pine needles change the answer

An independent test by This Old House of 12 guard systems against pine needles found micro-mesh the only type that reliably kept them out, with brush and reverse-curve guards the worst ([This Old House](https://thisoldhouse.com/gutters/best-gutter-guards-for-pine-needles)). That matches what we see around Camberley, Sandhurst, Yateley and Hawley, where needles fall all year and knit together. If you are under pine, it is micro-mesh or nothing; see [pine needles in gutters](https://www.gutterteam.co.uk/advice/pine-needles-in-gutters/).

## Under oak, beech and horse chestnut

Broadleaf trees drop most of their load in one go, from October to early December. Mesh and brush guards stop the big leaves getting in, but wet leaves can mat on top of the guard and send water over the front edge in a downpour. Plan to clear the top after the leaf drop finishes. See [how often to clean gutters](https://www.gutterteam.co.uk/advice/how-often-clean-gutters/).

## What guards cannot do

- **Stop downpipe blockages completely.** Fine silt and moss from the roof still reach the outlet.
- **Fix a gutter that has lost its fall.** If water sits in the run, a guard just hides it.
- **Make a cast iron system maintenance-free.** Guards can hold moisture against old iron. See [cast iron gutters](https://www.gutterteam.co.uk/advice/cast-iron-gutters/).
- **Remove the need to look.** Birds nest on brush and foam guards, and moss grows on all of them.

## Cost

Published UK guides put supply-only prices at roughly £2 to £4 a metre for budget plastic mesh, £6 to £12 for aluminium mesh and £12 to £20 for steel micro-mesh ([Pure Seal Services](https://puresealservices.co.uk/are-gutter-guards-worth-it-insights-from-professional-roofers/)). Fitting is extra and needs the same safe access as a clean. These are market estimates, not quotes.

## Our view

For a house on an open estate with a single clean a year, guards rarely pay back. For a house under pine, good micro-mesh fitted after a thorough clean can cut call-outs, but it still needs a yearly check. Whatever you decide, have the gutters and downpipes cleared and flow-tested first: fitting a guard over a blocked gutter locks the problem in.
